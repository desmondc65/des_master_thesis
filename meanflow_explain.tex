\noindent The five equations on slides 30--34 build a single argument:
\emph{define} the object we want $\rightarrow$ show \emph{why} it is useful
$\rightarrow$ expose the \emph{catch} $\rightarrow$ make it \emph{computable}
$\rightarrow$ \emph{train} a network on it. The three-panel figure
(Diffusion $\approx 35$ calls / FlowCast $\approx 10$ / MeanFlow $1$) stays
fixed while the equation underneath advances.

% ==================================================================
\section*{Slide 30 --- Average velocity (definition)}
\begin{equation*}
  u(z_r, r, t) \;\triangleq\; \frac{1}{t - r} \int_{r}^{t} v(z_\tau, \tau)\, d\tau
\end{equation*}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{The picture on the right is the whole story:} diffusion
        curves to the forecast in $\approx 35$ calls, FlowCast straightens it
        to $\approx 10$, MeanFlow wants to jump there in \textbf{1}.
  \item $v(z_\tau, \tau)$ is the \textbf{instantaneous} velocity FlowCast
        already learns --- the local tangent at each point of the path.
  \item $u$ is its \textbf{time-average over the interval $[r, t]$}:
        displacement divided by interval length. It is the single constant
        velocity that carries you from $z_r$ to $z_t$.
  \item Crucially, $u$ is a \textbf{property of the flow itself}, not of any
        network --- and as $t \to r$ it collapses back to $v$
        (one evaluation $=$ FlowCast).
\end{itemize}

% ==================================================================
\section*{Slide 31 --- Single-step transport (why we want $u$)}
\begin{equation*}
  z_t = z_r + (t - r)\, u(z_r, r, t)
\end{equation*}
\begin{itemize}
  \item This is just the definition rearranged --- but read it as a
        \textbf{sampler}: knowing $u$ turns transport into \textbf{one
        algebraic step}, no ODE solver.
  \item Set $(r, t) = (0, 1)$ and a \textbf{single evaluation maps
        noise $\rightarrow$ forecast} (the green dot, one call).
  \item This is where MeanFlow differs from FlowCast in kind, not degree:
        FlowCast \emph{integrates} many small steps; MeanFlow takes
        \textbf{one exact step}.
  \item \textbf{The catch:} $u$ is defined by an intractable path integral
        (slide 30), so we cannot regress a network onto it directly. The cost
        has moved from inference into training.
\end{itemize}

% ==================================================================
\section*{Slide 32 --- The MeanFlow identity \textnormal{(boxed = key result)}}
\begin{equation*}
  u(z_r, r, t) \;=\; v(z_r, r) \;+\; (t - r)\, \frac{d}{dr}\, u(z_r, r, t)
\end{equation*}
\begin{itemize}
  \item We \textbf{kill the integral by differentiating instead of
        integrating.} Multiply slide 30 by $(t - r)$, differentiate in the
        state-time $r$ (product rule $+$ fundamental theorem of calculus).
  \item Result relates the field we \textbf{want} ($u$) to the field we
        \textbf{can sample} ($v$), using only quantities at a \textbf{single
        point of a single trajectory} --- the integral is gone.
  \item The term $(t - r)\, \frac{d}{dr} u$ is the \textbf{correction} that
        lifts the local velocity $v$ to the interval average $u$.
  \item At $t = r$ it reduces to $u = v$ --- \textbf{plain flow matching is
        the boundary condition}, which is exactly why MeanFlow is a clean
        generalisation of FlowCast.
\end{itemize}

% ==================================================================
\section*{Slide 33 --- Expanding the total derivative}
\begin{equation*}
  \frac{d}{dr}\, u(z_r, r, t) \;=\; v(z_r, r)\, \partial_z u \;+\; \partial_r u
\end{equation*}
\begin{itemize}
  \item The $\frac{d}{dr}$ in the identity is a \textbf{total} derivative ---
        the state $z_r$ itself moves along the trajectory as $r$ changes
        ($dz_r / dr = v$).
  \item Chain rule splits it into two \textbf{partials we can compute}:
        motion through space ($v\, \partial_z u$) plus the explicit dependence
        on the state-time ($\partial_r u$). ($t$ is held fixed, so no
        $\partial_t$ term.)
  \item This is the form that maps directly onto a \textbf{Jacobian--vector
        product (JVP)}: one extra forward-mode pass with tangent $(v, 1, 0)$
        for $(z, r, t)$ --- no higher-order gradients.
  \item In short: this line is what makes the identity \textbf{actually
        trainable} on a network.
\end{itemize}

% ==================================================================
\section*{Slide 34 --- Training objective}
\begin{align*}
  \mathcal{L}_{\text{MF}}
  &= \Ebb\Bigl[\, \mathrm{sg}(w)\,
       \bigl\| u_\theta(z_r, r, t, c_t) - \mathrm{sg}(u_{\text{tgt}}) \bigr\|_{2,\beta}^2
     \,\Bigr] + \lambda_{\text{spec}}\, \mathcal{L}_{\text{spec}}, \\[4pt]
  u_{\text{tgt}} &= v + (t - r)\bigl( v\, \partial_z u_\theta + \partial_r u_\theta \bigr)
\end{align*}
\begin{itemize}
  \item We \textbf{regress the network onto the identity's right-hand side}:
        the target $u_{\text{tgt}}$ is just slide 32 with slide 33 substituted
        in, and the network \textbf{bootstraps its own derivative}.
  \item \textbf{$c_t$ is the conditioning bundle} the network sees alongside
        the noisy state --- $c_t = \mathrm{concat}(X_{t-1},\, M_t,\, I)$:
        the previous high-res state $X_{t-1}$, the frozen regression mean
        $M_t$ (the deterministic forecast this residual is correcting), and the
        static invariants $I$ (land--sea mask, orography). It is concatenated
        channel-wise with $z_r$ as network input. The low-res ERA5 field $S_t$
        is \emph{deliberately omitted} from the direct path --- its synoptic
        signal already enters through $M_t = \Freg(X_{t-1}, S_t, I)$. The same
        $c_t$ is used by EDM, FlowCast, and MeanFlow, so only the generative
        trajectory differs across heads.
  \item $v = x_1 - x_0$ is the conditional I-CFM velocity (same substitution
        FlowCast makes, valid in expectation) --- \textbf{still teacher-free,
        no diffusion model queried}.
  \item \textbf{stop-gradient ($\mathrm{sg}$)} on the target and on the
        weight: we do not backprop through the JVP, so training stays
        first-order and stable.
  \item \textbf{$w = (\|\Delta\|_{2,\beta}^2 + \varepsilon)^{-p}$} is adaptive
        loss weighting (down-weights hard samples); \textbf{$\|\cdot\|_{2,\beta}$}
        carries the same per-channel weights $\beta = (1, 1, 1, 2)$ and
        \textbf{$\mathcal{L}_{\text{spec}}$} the same \texttt{qpepre} spectral
        term as FlowCast.
  \item A network at zero loss \textbf{satisfies the identity $\rightarrow$
        equals the true average velocity.} And on the $75\%$ of the batch where
        $t = r$, $u_{\text{tgt}} = v$ and this is \textbf{literally the
        FlowCast loss} --- so MeanFlow vs FlowCast is an A/B on the objective
        alone.
\end{itemize}

% ------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection*{What is $\mathcal{L}_{\text{spec}}$? (the spectral regulariser)}
\begin{equation*}
  \mathcal{L}_{\text{spec}} = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{K}|}\sum_{\kappa \in \mathcal{K}}
    \bigl|\log P_{\text{pred}}(\kappa) - \log P_{\text{target}}(\kappa)\bigr|
\end{equation*}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{What it measures:} the L1 distance between the
        \textbf{radially-averaged log power spectra} of the predicted and
        target \texttt{qpepre} fields. $P(\kappa)$ is the 2D power spectral
        density averaged over all directions at wavenumber $\kappa$
        (i.e.\ ``how much energy lives at spatial scale $1/\kappa$''), and
        $\mathcal{K}$ is the set of non-zero radial wavenumbers on the grid.
  \item \textbf{Why we need it:} pixel-wise RMSE-type losses reward
        \textbf{smooth, low-amplitude} precipitation --- the model minimises
        error by predicting a ``slightly-wet-everywhere'' blur and throws away
        the sharp convective structure we actually care about. The spectral
        term penalises a \textbf{wrong spatial spectrum}, forcing the right
        amount of fine-scale (high-$\kappa$) energy back in.
  \item \textbf{Log of the PSD} because precipitation energy spans orders of
        magnitude across scales; the log makes the regulariser care about
        \emph{all} wavenumbers, not just the few most energetic ones.
  \item \textbf{Applied only to \texttt{qpepre}}, on the flow's
        \emph{implied clean residual} $\hat{x}_1 = z_r + (1 - r)\,u_\theta$ (not
        the raw velocity), and --- for MeanFlow --- only on the collapsed
        $t = r$ subset, so it stays directly comparable to the FlowCast term.
        Weighted by $\lambda_{\text{spec}} = 0.1$.
\end{itemize}

\section*{EXPERIMENTS}

\subsection*{E1 --- Setup / ``How we compare fairly''}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{One question:} can a teacher-free $1$--$2$-call model match the
        $35$-call diffusion baseline? Three heads, \textbf{everything else held
        fixed} --- same frozen regression mean, same SongUNet backbone, same
        $\sigma_{\text{data}}$, same cleaned log1p dataset.
  \item \textbf{Matched budget = training \emph{samples} seen ($\approx 2$\,M),
        not steps} (batch sizes differ: EDM 64, FlowCast 96, MeanFlow 112). So
        any gap reflects the \emph{generative trajectory}, not training compute.
  \item \textbf{Evaluation:} 2022 full validation year (8{,}759 hours),
        5-member ensembles, 10 initial times, $+1$\,h$\ldots+6$\,h rollouts.
        All scores denormalised to physical units (mm\,h$^{-1}$, K, m\,s$^{-1}$).
  \item \textbf{Caveat up front:} single-snapshot validation noise is
        $10$--$30\%$ --- only call gaps that clear that floor.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{E2 --- Headline scoreboard (the central result)}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{FlowCast ($K{=}10$) ties or beats diffusion at $3.7\times$ less
        cost:} CRPS $0.552$ vs $0.554$ (a tie), and clearly ahead on winds
        ($-5\%$ u10, $-12\%$ v10) and precipitation RMSE ($-7\%$).
  \item \textbf{MeanFlow ($K{=}2$) posts the best precip hit rates of any head}
        --- CSI-M $0.269$ and HSS-M $0.315$, both \emph{above} diffusion's
        $0.262$/$0.293$ --- at \textbf{$\approx 10\times$} less cost
        ($1.16$\,s vs $11.2$\,s per sequence).
  \item \textbf{The trade is coherent, not a wash:} FlowCast is the
        \emph{conservative} forecaster (lowest false alarms), diffusion and
        MeanFlow-$K2$ are more \emph{aggressive} (more hits, more false alarms).
        Which you want is an operational choice.
  \item \textbf{MeanFlow's one concession:} rollout CRPS lags by $\approx 10\%$
        --- a spread (calibration) gap, diagnosed later, not a skill gap.
  \item Suggested visual: the 4-row scoreboard table or
        \texttt{scoreboard\_3way.png}.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{E3 --- Inference cost (the ``money'' slide)}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{The cost that motivated the thesis:} a 50-member, 24\,h ensemble
        cycle. Diffusion does $50{\times}24{\times}35 = \mathbf{42{,}000}$
        network calls per cycle; MeanFlow-$K2$ does just \textbf{2{,}400}
        ($1{,}200$ at $K{=}1$).
  \item \textbf{Wall-clock (A6000):} EDM $\approx 31.6$\,min $\rightarrow$
        FlowCast-$K10$ $\approx 10.8$\,min ($2.9\times$) $\rightarrow$
        \textbf{MeanFlow-$K2$ $\approx 4.0$\,min ($7.9\times$)},
        MeanFlow-$K1$ $\approx 3.2$\,min ($9.9\times$).
  \item \textbf{Hardware-independent, and the gap \emph{widens} on faster
        silicon:} on the H100 the speedups grow to $3.1\times$ / $8.5\times$ /
        $\mathbf{10.8\times}$ (diffusion stays compute-bound on its many calls).
  \item \textbf{No second training stage:} these speedups are \emph{intrinsic}
        to the straight-line / average-velocity model --- no distillation, no
        teacher. ``A path worth turning twice: first straightened, then jumped.''
  \item Suggested visual: \texttt{timing\_speedup.png} (log-axis bars, both GPUs).
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{E4 --- Where the differences live (precip skill + rollout)}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Light/moderate rain:} flow heads have a clear false-alarm
        advantage (FlowCast FAR $0.20$ vs diffusion $0.29$ at $0.1$\,mm\,h$^{-1}$)
        --- they don't paint speculative drizzle on dry pixels.
  \item \textbf{Heavy rain ($\geq 5$\,mm\,h$^{-1}$) is hard for everyone} at
        this budget --- a genuine, honest weakness; neighbourhood pooling
        (recovering displacement) and longer training are the levers.
  \item \textbf{Rollout is stable:} all heads grow error monotonically, none
        blows up; the cleaned flow heads avoid the legacy baseline's unphysical
        \texttt{qpepre} excursion at $+4$\,h.
  \item \textbf{Qualitative case (2022-10-15, $\approx 80$\,mm\,h$^{-1}$ peak):}
        diffusion spreads broad rain, FlowCast damps the intense cores,
        \textbf{MeanFlow-$K2$ keeps the most high-intensity structure at 2 calls}
        --- and is \emph{not} visibly blurrier than FlowCast's 10-step fields.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{E5 --- Ablations (optional / backup)}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{log1p encoding is the single biggest preprocessing win:}
        compresses qpepre's std from $9.12$ to $\approx 0.37$ and improves
        \emph{every} channel ($\sim 20$--$25\%$ on t2m) --- the badly-scaled raw
        channel otherwise contaminates the shared U-Net trunk.
  \item \textbf{Precip weight $w_{\text{pr}}$:} no clean monotone trend;
        $w_{\text{pr}}{=}2.0$ is the all-round default (best t2m, competitive
        qpepre), $1.4$ is the wind sweet spot. MeanFlow reuses $2.0$ unchanged
        so the A/B stays clean.
  \item \textbf{Legacy $\rightarrow$ cleaned pipeline} is a large step on its
        own (CRPS $0.63{\to}0.55$, CSI-M $0.14{\to}0.26$), \emph{independent} of
        the head --- MeanFlow adds a $17\times$ end-to-end speedup on top.
\end{itemize}

% ------------------------------------------------------------------
\section*{DISCUSSION}

\subsection*{D1 --- What the evidence says}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Answer to the thesis question: a qualified ``yes.''} At
        $1$--$2$ calls MeanFlow reaches the diffusion baseline's deterministic
        \emph{and} categorical skill class, with the best precip hit rates and
        near-best temperature, at $\approx 1/10$ the cost.
  \item \textbf{FlowCast marks the intermediate point on the same axis:} the
        straight-line path already recovers most of the diffusion skill ($K{=}10$,
        $3.7\times$); the average-velocity jump removes the \emph{last} of the
        integration cost.
  \item \textbf{The one honest concession is calibration, not skill:}
        MeanFlow's rollout CRPS gap is a spread problem (next slide), most
        of it closed by the second segment.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{D2 --- Failure modes (be honest)}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Heavy-rain under-detection} --- all heads converge to low CSI
        above $5$\,mm\,h$^{-1}$ at $2$\,M samples; flow heads' conservatism
        costs hits.
  \item \textbf{MeanFlow under-dispersion at $K{=}1$} --- one average-velocity
        jump lands each noise draw near the conditional mean, narrowing
        ensemble spread; it \emph{compounds} under autoregression. $\Rightarrow$
        \textbf{$K{=}2$ is the recommended operating point.}
  \item \textbf{FlowCast \texttt{t2m} rollout drift} ($1.73$\,K vs $1.60$\,K at
        $+6$\,h) --- but MeanFlow does \emph{not} inherit it despite sharing the
        backbone, so it's a property of the learned \emph{trajectory}, not the
        architecture.
  \item \textbf{Three caveats bounding all claims:} single $2$\,M snapshot
        ($10$--$30\%$ noise); one regression mean / one domain (Taiwan); the
        log1p encoding must match between regression and residual head.
\end{itemize}

% ------------------------------------------------------------------
\section*{CONCLUSION}

\subsection*{C1 --- Contributions}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{First application of the MeanFlow objective to a weather
        forecaster:} a single-run, teacher-free average-velocity residual head
        for StormCast, sampling in $1$--$2$ calls with \emph{no} ODE solver.
  \item \textbf{A controlled three-head comparison} (EDM / FlowCast / MeanFlow)
        sharing regression mean, backbone, and $\sigma_{\text{data}}$ --- so
        MeanFlow-vs-FlowCast is an A/B on the training objective alone.
  \item \textbf{A precipitation-aware loss \& evaluation suite}
        (channel-weighted L2 $+$ radial log-PSD; RMSE/CRPS/CSI/FSS/HSS/spectral/
        rollout) plus log1p and $w_{\text{pr}}$ ablations.
  \item \textbf{The headline finding:} diffusion-class skill at a tenth of the
        cost, teacher-free.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{C2 --- Future work}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Train to convergence and re-score} (FlowCast already at
        $\approx 13.4$\,M) --- the single most important step to harden the
        provisional headline against snapshot noise.
  \item \textbf{Restore low-NFE rollout spread} --- stochastic single-segment
        sampler / noise re-injection / spread-aware loss, to make the true
        $1$--$2$-NFE regime probabilistically competitive end to end.
  \item \textbf{Close the heavy-rain gap} (tail-quantile / focal loss on
        \texttt{qpepre}) and \textbf{exploit cheap sampling for large-ensemble
        calibration} (50--100 members, rank histograms).
  \item \textbf{Isolate the loss recipe} (re-train EDM with the same rain-aware
        terms) and \textbf{test transfer} to another domain (HRRR/CONUS).
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{C3 --- Closing line (one slide, big text)}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Generative modelling is the right inductive bias for convective-scale
        forecasting; the \emph{multi-step sampler} was the obstacle.
  \item \textbf{The path from noise to weather is a dial worth turning twice:
        first straightened (FlowCast), then jumped (MeanFlow).}
  \item Sharp, probabilistic, regional forecasting \emph{fast enough to run
        between radar volumes} is now within reach --- with no teacher.
\end{itemize}
