\chapter{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{ch:conclusion}

\section{Summary of Contributions}
\label{sec:concl-summary}

This thesis asked whether a single-run, teacher-free flow-matching residual can replace the EDM diffusion residual in a regional, convection-permitting StormCast forecaster over the Taiwan domain --- delivering the speed a curved diffusion ODE cannot, without sacrificing the sharpness and probabilistic calibration that motivated generative forecasting in the first place. Starting from the StormCast two-stage decomposition (a frozen deterministic regression mean plus a generative residual head) trained on the Taiwan RWRF dataset, we adapted Conditional Flow Matching into \textbf{FlowCast}, a residual head that learns a straight-line noise-to-data velocity field and samples it with a handful of explicit Euler steps, and we compared it head-to-head against the EDM diffusion residual on the full 2022 hourly validation year.

The concrete contributions are: (i) a single-training-run FlowCast residual pipeline, anchored to exactly the same frozen regression mean, backbone, and $\sigma_{\text{data}}$ as the diffusion baseline so that the comparison isolates the generative trajectory; (ii) a precipitation-aware loss --- channel-weighted L2 plus a radial log-PSD regulariser on \texttt{qpepre} --- together with \texttt{log1p}-encoding and precipitation-weight ablations; (iii) a precipitation-aware evaluation suite spanning deterministic (RMSE/MAE), probabilistic (CRPS), categorical (CSI/FSS/HSS/FAR), spectral, and rollout metrics; and (iv) the resulting head-to-head finding that, at a matched $\approx 2\,\text{M}$-sample budget, FlowCast at 10 Euler steps matches the diffusion baseline's CRPS, beats it on wind and precipitation RMSE and on false-alarm ratio, and runs $\approx 2.9\times$ faster, while conceding some pixel-exact heavy-rain hit rate and some \texttt{t2m} accuracy.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:concl-limitations}

Several limitations should be kept in mind when interpreting these results.

\paragraph{Single training snapshot.} The headline comparison is taken at a single $\approx 2\,\text{M}$-sample checkpoint, where single-snapshot validation noise is $10$--$30\,\%$. The canonical FlowCast run continues to $\approx 13.4\,\text{M}$ samples, and the diffusion baseline likewise has more training available; a converged-budget comparison is not yet in hand.

\paragraph{Single regression mean and single domain.} Both heads are anchored to one frozen regression mean trained on one regional domain (Taiwan RWRF). We cannot assess transfer to other convection-permitting domains (HRRR/CONUS StormCast) without retraining, nor disentangle how much of FlowCast's behaviour depends on the specific quality of this regression mean.

\paragraph{Heavy-rain detection.} FlowCast's conservative, low-false-alarm behaviour costs pixel-exact hits at the heavier rainfall thresholds. The neighbourhood FSS recovers part of this, suggesting a displacement rather than a pure miss, but the deterministic heavy-rain CSI is a genuine weakness at the current budget.

\paragraph{Limited probabilistic evaluation.} CRPS is computed from a small per-hour ensemble. A large-ensemble calibration study (rank histograms, spread--skill) --- which a cheap few-step sampler makes affordable --- has not been run.

\paragraph{No few-step diffusion comparator.} We compare FlowCast against the full $\approx 25$-NFE diffusion sampler, not against a distilled few-step diffusion student. Whether a distilled diffusion model could close FlowCast's speed gap while keeping the diffusion baseline's heavy-rain CSI is an open question this thesis does not answer.

\section{Future Work}
\label{sec:concl-future}

The most promising directions, several of which are scoped concretely in the accompanying project to-do, are:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Train to convergence and re-score.} Run both heads to a matched, converged sample budget and repeat the full metric suite, smoothing over several validation snapshots to beat down the $10$--$30\,\%$ noise. This is the single most important step to turn the provisional headline into a defensible claim.
  \item \textbf{Close the heavy-rain gap.} Test an explicit tail-quantile or focal loss on \texttt{qpepre}, a higher $w_{\text{pr}}$ paired with the spectral term, and a stronger \texttt{asinh}/\texttt{log1p} dynamic-range transform, targeting the $5$--$10$\,mm\,h$^{-1}$ CSI deficit without inflating false alarms.
  \item \textbf{One- and two-step FlowCast.} Push the NFE sweep to $K \in \{1, 2\}$ and quantify exactly how much quality the straight-line path retains at the extreme low-step limit, where the operational payoff is largest.
  \item \textbf{Stochastic-interpolant / bridge variants.} Generalise the straight-line flow to an anchored stochastic-interpolant bridge that starts from the regression mean rather than white noise, which may shorten the effective transport distance and further sharpen precipitation.
  \item \textbf{Large-ensemble calibration.} Exploit FlowCast's low per-sample cost to run 50--100-member ensembles and report CRPS, rank histograms, and spread--skill --- the uncertainty-quantification payoff that cheap sampling unlocks.
  \item \textbf{Transfer to other domains.} Apply the same single-run FlowCast recipe to another convection-permitting regression mean (e.g.\ an HRRR-trained StormCast) to measure how much of the pipeline is Taiwan-specific.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Closing Remarks}
\label{sec:concl-closing}

Generative modelling is almost certainly the right inductive bias for short-range convective-scale forecasting; the obstacle to deploying it has been the multi-step diffusion sampler. This thesis shows that, on a real regional dataset, a straight-line flow-matching residual can recover most of the diffusion baseline's skill --- and exceed it on winds, precipitation RMSE, and false alarms --- at roughly a third of the inference cost, trained in a single run with no teacher. The remaining gaps (heavy-rain hits, \texttt{t2m} drift, convergence at a larger budget) are concrete and addressable. Making sharp, probabilistic, regional convective forecasting fast enough to run between radar volumes is within reach, and a single-stage flow-matching residual is a credible path to it.
